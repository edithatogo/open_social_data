# What this resource can and cannot tell us

This project will connect public records of young people's deaths with carefully
chosen economic and policy information. At present it contains a catalogue and
research plans, not downloaded mortality records or results.

A lower rate after a policy change does not by itself show that the policy caused
the change. We must compare appropriate populations, check other changes, and
make uncertainty visible. Countries can record deaths differently; a missing
entry does not mean nobody died.

Some questions can use public totals. Questions about bereaved families, treatment
or school attendance need additional studies or approved confidential data. Those
records must never be uploaded into the public catalogue. Published small-number
protections must remain intact.

The aim is to identify prevention and information investments that improve lives,
not to rank individual children by risk or reduce the value of a child's life to
future earnings. Youth, families, relevant communities and service teams should
help shape each substantive study and its reporting.
